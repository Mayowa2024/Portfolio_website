## Overview

This technical literature review examines the role of artificial intelligence in autonomous vehicles, with particular attention to the robustness of perception systems and the barriers affecting safe, widespread deployment.

The review evaluates competing perspectives across engineering and society. It connects the technical limitations of AI perception with questions of safety validation, regulation, ethics, public trust, and real-world operational constraints.

## Topics Covered

- The contested impact of autonomous vehicles on transportation and society
- Technological, safety, legal, regulatory, social, and ethical barriers
- AI-perception failure under adverse weather and unfamiliar scenarios
- Generalisation and adversarial vulnerability in deep-learning systems
- LiDAR-centric and vision-only perception strategies
- Explainable AI and standardised validation for autonomous-driving systems

## Technical Perspective

The paper argues that perception robustness is a central constraint on autonomous-vehicle safety. It considers how cameras, LiDAR, radar, sensor fusion, machine learning, and deep learning contribute to environmental understanding, while also examining the limitations that emerge outside controlled benchmarks and familiar operating conditions.
