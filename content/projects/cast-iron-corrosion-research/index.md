## Overview

**Full project title:** Investigation of the Potential of Human Urine as an Organic Corrosion Inhibitor for Cast Iron in Sodium Chloride Media  
**Project type:** Undergraduate Mechanical Engineering Research Project  
**Institution:** Federal University of Agriculture Abeokuta (FUNAAB), Nigeria  
**Status:** Completed

This experimental research project investigated the corrosion behaviour of cast iron in a sodium chloride (NaCl) environment and evaluated an aqueous-ammonia inhibitor derived from urine as a low-cost approach to corrosion control. The work combined controlled specimen exposure, quantitative corrosion analysis, adsorption modelling, surface characterisation, and engineering interpretation to assess whether an inexpensive, readily available organic feedstock could support cast-iron corrosion control in saline conditions.

## Research Problem

Cast iron exposed to saline environments is susceptible to corrosion, which can progressively degrade material performance and service life. Commercial corrosion-control methods can add cost or be difficult to access, creating a need to investigate practical alternatives.

The study examined whether the selected organic material could inhibit cast-iron corrosion in NaCl solution. Its performance was evaluated by comparing inhibited specimens with an uninhibited control and analysing how inhibitor concentration and exposure time affected corrosion behaviour.

## Methodology

The research followed an experimental and comparative approach:

- Prepared cast-iron specimens for controlled corrosion testing.
- Exposed specimens to NaCl environments with and without the organic inhibitor.
- Used the uninhibited condition as the control for performance comparison.
- Calculated and analysed corrosion rates and inhibition efficiencies.
- Investigated the effects of inhibitor concentration and exposure time.
- Evaluated adsorption behaviour at the cast-iron surface.
- Applied quantitative and graphical methods to analyse the experimental measurements.

## Experimental Analysis

Measurements from the inhibited and control conditions were compared to determine the extent to which the organic inhibitor reduced material degradation. The analysis considered changes in corrosion rate and inhibition efficiency across the experimental conditions, providing a quantitative basis for evaluating corrosion-control performance.

MATLAB and Python supported the calculation workflow and scientific visualisation of the experimental data. The results were interpreted from both materials-engineering and corrosion-engineering perspectives rather than treated solely as numerical outputs.

## Key Results

| Test condition | Corrosion rate |
| --- | ---: |
| Organic-inhibitor condition | Approximately **0.166 mm/year** |
| Uninhibited control | Approximately **0.264 mm/year** |

The inhibited cast-iron sample exhibited a lower corrosion rate than the uninhibited control. This result demonstrated a meaningful reduction in corrosion under the evaluated saline conditions and supported further consideration of the organic material as a low-cost corrosion inhibitor.

## Adsorption Modelling

Adsorption behaviour was examined to understand how the inhibitor interacted with the cast-iron surface. The experimental data were modelled using three established adsorption isotherms:

- Langmuir adsorption isotherm
- Freundlich adsorption isotherm
- Frumkin adsorption isotherm

Gibbs free-energy calculations were also used to investigate the nature of inhibitor adsorption on the material surface. Together, the isotherm models and thermodynamic analysis provided a framework for interpreting the corrosion-inhibition mechanism.

## Engineering Significance

The project demonstrates a complete experimental engineering workflow: defining a materials-degradation problem, establishing controlled comparison conditions, collecting and analysing measurements, testing explanatory models, and translating the findings into an assessment of engineering performance.

Beyond the measured reduction in corrosion rate, the research explored the feasibility of using an inexpensive and readily available organic material for corrosion control. It also developed practical experience in corrosion science, experimental design, materials evaluation, quantitative modelling, and formal technical reporting.
